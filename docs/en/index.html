New from the workshop

Honda CB250G · Rebuild under way

The frame has been powder-coated, and the freshly rebuilt engine is back in place: new pistons, a rebored and honed cylinder, lapped valves, a new timing chain and adjusted valve clearances.

Now the careful reassembly begins. Missing parts still need to be found; the tank and side covers are waiting for Hawaiian Blue Metallic. The motorcycle came from Denmark but has Swiss registration papers, so customs clearance is also part of the project.

  1. Frame & running gear prepared
  2. Engine rebuilt & installed
  3. Fittings & missing parts
  4. Paint, customs & final assembly

Next step: Sort the fittings, find the missing parts and prepare the next stages of assembly.

06 · The idea behind it

Preserving the old. Looking closely.

This website brings together things that have stayed with me for many years: classic motorcycles, their technology and the small wonders of nature.

From 1998 to 2010, I lived near Durban in South Africa. That is where my macro photography began: often, stepping out of the living room was enough to be surrounded by an astonishingly varied world of insects. Until 2014, I tried to make the small things large – animals and details that are otherwise easily overlooked. The most beautiful photographs were often taken very early in the morning, when dew still lay on the grass and the insects were cool and unhurried. Germany also has plenty to discover when one takes the time to look closely.

My motorcycles date roughly from 1950 to 1990 – from my own time, not from a distant past. I ride and restore them because their technology is still understandable, and because many parts can be repaired or even made anew on the lathe. Work in the workshop is a special balance for me: time disappears, as do many of the things that otherwise occupy my mind. And riding is not about speed. When the engine is running smoothly and sounding right at around 80 km/h, that is already a very good day for me.
